Land and home values in Alaska

Across Alaska's 30 counties, residential land is about $7.46 per sq ft at the state median ($324,783 an acre). The typical home is worth about $394,000 and a 2-bedroom rents for about $1,540 a month. Largest city: Anchorage; capital: Juneau. Values as of October 12, 2026.

Questions people ask

How much is land worth in Alaska?

Residential land in Alaska is about $7.46 per square foot, or $324,783 an acre, as of October 2026. A quarter-acre lot (10,890 sq ft) is about $81,196. Smaller lots sell for more per square foot and large tracts for less.

Where is land most expensive in Alaska?

The highest county medians for residential land are Sitka City and Borough ($19.70 per sq ft), Juneau City and Borough ($19.30 per sq ft), Anchorage Municipality ($14.90 per sq ft), Ketchikan Gateway Borough ($11.90 per sq ft).

Where is land cheapest in Alaska?

The lowest county medians are Kusilvak Census Area ($0.78 per sq ft), Yukon-Koyukuk Census Area ($0.95 per sq ft), Dillingham Census Area ($0.98 per sq ft), Nome Census Area ($1.37 per sq ft); farmland and remote acreage are cheaper still.

What is the typical home value in Alaska?

The typical (median) home in Alaska is worth about $394,000, with entry-level homes around $278,000 and upper-end homes around $509,000, as of October 2026.

How much is rent in Alaska?

A 2-bedroom rents for about $1,540 a month in Alaska, a 1-bedroom about $1,200 and a 3-bedroom about $2,130 (gross rent including utilities, HUD Fair Market Rent basis).
