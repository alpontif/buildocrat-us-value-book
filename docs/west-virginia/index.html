Land and home values in West Virginia

Across West Virginia's 55 counties, residential land is about $3.02 per sq ft at the state median ($131,333 an acre). The typical home is worth about $185,000 and a 2-bedroom rents for about $980 a month. Largest city: Charleston; capital: Charleston. Values as of October 12, 2026.

Questions people ask

How much is land worth in West Virginia?

Residential land in West Virginia is about $3.02 per square foot, or $131,333 an acre, as of October 2026. A quarter-acre lot (10,890 sq ft) is about $32,833. Smaller lots sell for more per square foot and large tracts for less.

Where is land most expensive in West Virginia?

The highest county medians for residential land are Jefferson County ($6.07 per sq ft), Monongalia County ($6.04 per sq ft), Berkeley County ($5.59 per sq ft), Putnam County ($4.76 per sq ft).

Where is land cheapest in West Virginia?

The lowest county medians are McDowell County ($0.37 per sq ft), Gilmer County ($0.60 per sq ft), Webster County ($0.70 per sq ft), Wyoming County ($0.73 per sq ft); farmland and remote acreage are cheaper still.

What is the typical home value in West Virginia?

The typical (median) home in West Virginia is worth about $185,000, with entry-level homes around $109,000 and upper-end homes around $275,000, as of October 2026.

How much is rent in West Virginia?

A 2-bedroom rents for about $980 a month in West Virginia, a 1-bedroom about $805 and a 3-bedroom about $1,280 (gross rent including utilities, HUD Fair Market Rent basis).

How much is farmland per acre in West Virginia?

Farmland averages about $3,640 an acre in West Virginia (USDA, including buildings), well below residential land.
