Land and home values in North Dakota

Across North Dakota's 53 counties, residential land is about $5.67 per sq ft at the state median ($246,898 an acre). The typical home is worth about $289,000 and a 2-bedroom rents for about $1,080 a month. Largest city: Fargo; capital: Bismarck. Values as of October 12, 2026.

Questions people ask

How much is land worth in North Dakota?

Residential land in North Dakota is about $5.67 per square foot, or $246,898 an acre, as of October 2026. A quarter-acre lot (10,890 sq ft) is about $61,725. Smaller lots sell for more per square foot and large tracts for less.

Where is land most expensive in North Dakota?

The highest county medians for residential land are Bottineau County ($21.10 per sq ft), Williams County ($9.62 per sq ft), McKenzie County ($8.91 per sq ft), Cass County ($7.28 per sq ft).

Where is land cheapest in North Dakota?

The lowest county medians are Dickey County ($0.46 per sq ft), Sargent County ($0.54 per sq ft), McIntosh County ($0.69 per sq ft), Sioux County ($0.73 per sq ft); farmland and remote acreage are cheaper still.

What is the typical home value in North Dakota?

The typical (median) home in North Dakota is worth about $289,000, with entry-level homes around $193,000 and upper-end homes around $398,000, as of October 2026.

How much is rent in North Dakota?

A 2-bedroom rents for about $1,080 a month in North Dakota, a 1-bedroom about $910 and a 3-bedroom about $1,500 (gross rent including utilities, HUD Fair Market Rent basis).

How much is farmland per acre in North Dakota?

Farmland averages about $2,450 an acre in North Dakota (USDA, including buildings), well below residential land.
