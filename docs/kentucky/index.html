Land and home values in Kentucky

Across Kentucky's 120 counties, residential land is about $3.73 per sq ft at the state median ($162,348 an acre). The typical home is worth about $220,000 and a 2-bedroom rents for about $1,100 a month. Largest city: Louisville/Jefferson County; capital: Frankfort. Values as of October 19, 2026.

Questions people ask

How much is land worth in Kentucky?

Residential land in Kentucky is about $3.73 per square foot, or $162,348 an acre, as of October 2026. A quarter-acre lot (10,890 sq ft) is about $40,587. Smaller lots sell for more per square foot and large tracts for less.

Where is land most expensive in Kentucky?

The highest county medians for residential land are Lyon County ($8.95 per sq ft), Fayette County ($8.72 per sq ft), Campbell County ($7.38 per sq ft), Oldham County ($6.81 per sq ft).

Where is land cheapest in Kentucky?

The lowest county medians are Letcher County ($0.44 per sq ft), Harlan County ($0.45 per sq ft), Leslie County ($0.45 per sq ft), Breathitt County ($0.51 per sq ft); farmland and remote acreage are cheaper still.

What is the typical home value in Kentucky?

The typical (median) home in Kentucky is worth about $220,000, with entry-level homes around $144,000 and upper-end homes around $311,000, as of October 2026.

How much is rent in Kentucky?

A 2-bedroom rents for about $1,100 a month in Kentucky, a 1-bedroom about $910 and a 3-bedroom about $1,440 (gross rent including utilities, HUD Fair Market Rent basis).

How much is farmland per acre in Kentucky?

Farmland averages about $5,600 an acre in Kentucky (USDA, including buildings), well below residential land.
