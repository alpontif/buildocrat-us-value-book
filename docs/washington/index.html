Land and home values in Washington

Across Washington's 39 counties, residential land is about $18.40 per sq ft at the state median ($801,504 an acre). The typical home is worth about $583,000 and a 2-bedroom rents for about $2,100 a month. Largest city: Seattle; capital: Olympia. Values as of October 12, 2026.

Questions people ask

How much is land worth in Washington?

Residential land in Washington is about $18.40 per square foot, or $801,504 an acre, as of October 2026. A quarter-acre lot (10,890 sq ft) is about $200,376. Smaller lots sell for more per square foot and large tracts for less.

Where is land most expensive in Washington?

The highest county medians for residential land are King County ($49.20 per sq ft), Snohomish County ($28.50 per sq ft), San Juan County ($27.30 per sq ft), Pend Oreille County ($20.70 per sq ft).

Where is land cheapest in Washington?

The lowest county medians are Garfield County ($2.50 per sq ft), Lincoln County ($5.49 per sq ft), Columbia County ($5.77 per sq ft), Adams County ($6.28 per sq ft); farmland and remote acreage are cheaper still.

What is the typical home value in Washington?

The typical (median) home in Washington is worth about $583,000, with entry-level homes around $439,000 and upper-end homes around $750,000, as of October 2026.

How much is rent in Washington?

A 2-bedroom rents for about $2,100 a month in Washington, a 1-bedroom about $1,740 and a 3-bedroom about $2,830 (gross rent including utilities, HUD Fair Market Rent basis).

How much is farmland per acre in Washington?

Farmland averages about $3,800 an acre in Washington (USDA, including buildings), well below residential land.
